Context

I grew up learning to respect the environment: my mother would take me into the forest from a young age, teaching me the names of flowers, trees, plants. That stayed with me. When the time came to choose an internship, finding a studio whose values matched mine was not optional, it was the whole point.

After a long search, I found 16 Mammals: a Dutch retail design agency in Amsterdam built around one conviction, that retail environments can and must operate within the carrying capacity of the planet. I moved to Amsterdam for three months, alone, to work alongside Rick Porcelijn and his team on real projects for real clients.

Each project pushed me to think about design not just as form and function, but as a chain of material decisions with a measurable ecological cost. It was the most formative three months of my studies.

About 16 Mammals

16 Mammals is a Dutch retail design agency based in Amsterdam. Their work spans the full store experience: from the overall spatial strategy and psychological routing down to material sourcing and inventory logic. The goal is to make retail future-proof, within the carrying capacity of natural resources.

The studio operates under the philosophy "Think Big Act Now", pioneering research into sustainable interior materials and mapping the hidden ecological impact of store design in a fact-based way. Clients range from vegan start-ups to established names such as Albert Heijn, Etos, AKO, and Pearle.

Founded and led by Rick Porcelijn, 16 Mammals works through a process that starts with a quick sketch and ends with a fully coordinated build, always balancing creative ambition with the practical constraints of builders, suppliers, and the planet.

"Our dot on the horizon is to help Food Retail Netherlands transform into a system that functions within the carrying capacity of the Earth. Not one, but all supermarkets eco-positive and accessible to all Dutch people."

Rick Porcelijn Founder & Creative Director

Bruna

Shop-in-shop concept for the Dutch bookseller's new space at Amsterdam Schiphol. The brief: find the right creative product range, design the identity and atmosphere of the unit, and present it to Bruna's team. Day-long store research across Amsterdam, moodboards for Molotov and Bruynzeel, spatial sketches and full V-Ray renders.

La BAB

Retail space for the Brasserie Artisanale de Bourgogne: a Burgundy winery that turned to craft beer after losing 60% of its harvest to frost. Brief: a wine and beer shop in Amsterdam, vintage, industrial, authentic. Designed across two levels, from the sun-lit ground floor to a cave-like basement, with full 3D renders and material research.

The Meal Shop

Two-week sprint for James Fresco's investor pitch: a fully automated dark kitchen where technology is the spectacle. Two propositions: a central hologram setup drawing attention to the open kitchen, and a corridor exhibition where digital canvases guide customers through the space. Less than 15 minutes wait, no seats, fully automated.

De Baronie Bridge

Structural and aesthetic design for a covered bridge connecting the car park to De Baronie's chocolate factory. Constraints: cost-efficient, durable, sustainable. Developed through collaborative sketching sessions with Rick, supported by reference research and moodboards for the final client presentation.

What it changed

01

Learned to calculate the carbon footprint of an entire supermarket: every shelf, every cashier, every material choice in a spreadsheet that gives a total impact score.

02

Opened up to tools I had never used: Rhino 7 for complex 3D modelling, V-Ray for photorealistic renders, and a new way of drawing, faster and cleaner, mixing liner sketches with Photoshop edits.

03

Managed a real client relationship independently for the first time, from brief to presentation. Learned to defend a creative direction, not just execute one.

04

Discovered the Gantt chart. Still use it. Rick has been tracking every working hour since 2012, by client, by project. That kind of discipline changed the way I approach my own work.

"I expect to create my own agency one day, and live my passion independently, as Rick Porcelijn does today."

From the internship report conclusion, Dec 2022
